## Design Specificity Verdict

An unrelated streaming app could not ship this UI unchanged, but could ship most of it reshaped. Specificity lives in the data layer and copy, not the visual language: lib/formatMedia.ts parses the station's own file convention (256_tema_predicador_01-02-2026) and resolves the preacher by clerical title; lib/time.ts:6 anchors everything to America/Bogota; prayer.tsx:290-300 gates submission on a Colombian data-protection consent.

What is not specific is the surface. constants/theme.ts:1-7 documents migrating mobile off the amber dial onto the public web's indigo so both front-ends match — defensible and cleanly executed, but the result reads as "the website in a phone shell". No Bogota in the visual layer. The two genuinely authored objects (the vinyl dial with progress ring, the CINTA filmstrip carousel) are broadcast-console aesthetic, not station identity.

Against the brief: the app already has competent, restrained motion. It does not have authored motion. No transition in the codebase was designed as a signature; it is 46-49 copy-pasted FadeInDown presets. That is the gap, and it is a design gap, not a library gap.

## What's Working

**components/player/DialVivo.tsx** — the best component in the repo and the reason the app already feels considered. The only place motion is authored rather than applied: three layers (SVG ring via useAnimatedProps on strokeDashoffset at :150-153, breathing halo at :92-114, rotating disc at :134-139) composed so each carries different information. Also the only component subscribing to reduceMotionChanged (:52-54) so it responds live; cancels loops on AppState !== active (:117-132); distinguishes song change from progress tick and resets the ring only on the former (:79-89); eases over 900ms to hide the poll's 5s granularity.

**components/PlayerControls.tsx** — AnimatedPressable with withTiming into shared values (:91-92, :109-110, :132-133) means press feedback never touches React state. The favourite micro-burst (:43-47) uses withSequence(withSpring(1.32, Spring.bouncy), withSpring(1, Spring.gentle)) and fires in both directions, because the comment at :52 correctly identifies the tap as the emotional beat, not the state.

**constants/theme.ts** — Colors.tally reserved exclusively for on-air state; Colors.warning for exactly one concept. tabular-nums on mono/monoLarge (:124-125) so no number in the app jitters.

## Priority Issues

### P0 — Reduce Motion is honoured backwards

49 layout-animation props (entering 30, exiting 12, layout 7) across 12 files, and zero read AccessibilityInfo.isReduceMotionEnabled. Five components do query it (_layout.tsx:52, DialVivo.tsx:48, NextUpCard.tsx:19, NowPlayingInfo.tsx:17, LiveBadge.tsx:16) but all five guard only continuous motion.

Why it matters: the model is inverted. Reduce Motion exists to suppress vestibular triggers, which are displacement and scaling; opacity is the safe subset. Today a Reduce Motion user loses the vinyl and halo (harmless, slow, ambient) and receives all 49 slide+fade entrances at full strength, including FadeInDown.delay(160 + idx * 40) on five social cards (social.tsx:155) reaching 320ms of delay before starting.

Fix: one hooks/useReducedMotion.ts returning the boolean plus a change subscription, and a pre-resolved transition map (enter: reduceMotion ? FadeIn.duration(120) : FadeInDown.duration(Durations.normal).easing(Easings.enter)). Replace the 49 inline literals. Delete the 5 duplicated effects and the dead tokens in the same change.

Command: harden

### P1 — The most important transition in the app is a hard cut

app/(tabs)/index.tsx returns three entirely separate trees — isLoading (:278-299), error (:301-329), live player (:331-481) — with no shared element, no key, no transition. Only loading and error carry entering (:285, :291, :304). The moment the stream comes up, the skeleton tree is torn out and a different tree appears instantly.

Fix: explicit hero entrance in the playing state — FadeIn.duration(260) on the DialVivo wrapper plus withSpring(0.94 → 1, Spring.gentle) on a scale shared value, NowPlayingInfo at FadeIn.delay(80).duration(260) so the dial leads. Max 320ms end-to-end.

## Questions to Consider
1. DialVivo already runs three simultaneous infinite animations on the main screen (9s rotation, 2.8s halo, 900ms progress ease) plus LiveBadge's 900ms loop. Is the app already over budget on a 2GB Android, in which case adding motion must be funded by removing motion first? Nobody has measured it, and lib/perf.ts sits unused for exactly this.
2. constants/motion.ts was written as a motion system and is now 60% dead. Was that intentional or did the system simply get lost? The answer decides whether the P0 and token fixes are one refactor or thirty edits.
3. _layout.tsx:104 opens on "Redes". Deliberate funnel to Facebook Live, or residue from when the Facebook banner was the primary event? If deliberate, the app's central emotional claim is deliberately off the first screen.
4. index.tsx:301-329 removes the play button when metadata fails. For a radio played in the background while someone works, is losing the controls on a transient blip acceptable? That fix is a product decision, not a design one.
5. useAudioPlayer exposes no amplitude, level, or PlaybackProgressUpdated data — the dial ring is fed by the 5s metadata poll, not the audio. Is a real waveform or level-reactive visual on the table? It would need new plumbing in hooks/useAudioPlayer.ts and is the one thing that would make the player unmistakably premium.
